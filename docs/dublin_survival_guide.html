📚 How Dublin homeless services work
Important: a day centre is not the same thing as a guaranteed bed. For emergency accommodation, contact the Dublin Homeless Freephone first.

DRHE / Dublin City Council — emergency accommodation

The Dublin Region Homeless Executive (DRHE), led by Dublin City Council, coordinates the regional response. The Homeless Freephone 1800 707 707 operates 10am–10pm, 7 days a week for emergency accommodation access in the Dublin Region.

If you are newly presenting as homeless in the Dublin City Council area, the Central Placement Service assesses your situation. Parkgate Hall, 6–9 Conyngham Road, Dublin 8, is appointment-led; contact the service first. Men, women and families can be assessed through the Central Placement Service.

If you have already presented to CPS and completed the housing/support assessment, the Freephone is the route to ask about temporary accommodation options. Bed availability is managed through the regional placement system, so there is no honest way for a public app to promise a bed at a particular centre.

Rough sleepers can also contact the Dublin Simon outreach service on 01 872 0185: Monday–Friday 7am–1am; Saturday–Sunday 9am–1am.

Capuchin Day Centre

29 Bow Street, Dublin 7. Free meals, food parcels and support. Published DRHE service information lists Monday–Saturday food service, with breakfast in the morning and lunch in the afternoon; food parcels are Wednesday morning. Showers, clothing and medical services are also associated with the centre.

Merchants Quay Ireland (MQI)

Riverbank Open Access Centre, 13–14 Merchant’s Quay, Dublin 8. Adults experiencing homelessness can drop in without an appointment. MQI provides food, showers, advice, advocacy, health and harm-reduction services. Current published service information lists Monday–Friday 8am–2pm and 2pm–7pm, plus Sunday 8am–2pm; Saturday is closed for the homeless day service.

Tiglin at the Lighthouse

28 Pearse Street, Dublin 2. A day/evening service offering food, connection and practical support. Recent reporting describes breakfast around 8:30am and a busy dinner service around 7pm, with the café operating six days a week and closed Sunday. Because its public hours can change, the app deliberately flags the hours as check-before-travel.

Little Flower Penny Dinners

11 Meath Street, Dublin 8. Free nutritious lunch with dessert and tea Monday–Friday at 12 noon. No pre-booking is required and everyone in need is welcome.

Crosscare

Crosscare provides community food, welfare and housing supports. Service locations and hours change, so the safest route is to use its current contact information rather than treating an old directory entry as guaranteed. A Crosscare community café/food-bank service operates on Portland Row; support has also moved from the former Cathedral Street location.

Focus Ireland

The Focus Ireland Coffee Shop at 14A Eustace Street, Temple Bar, is open 365 days and provides food, a warm indoor environment, advice and information. Current published hours are split into daytime and evening service windows, with Wednesday starting later. It is primarily a food/support service, not a guaranteed accommodation service.

Health & harm reduction — SafetyNet / MQI

SafetyNet Primary Care provides free GP and nursing care for people facing barriers to mainstream healthcare, including people experiencing homelessness. Its open-access hub is at 60 Amiens Street, Dublin 1, with walk-in clinics Monday–Friday 1:30–4pm and appointment clinics in the morning. SafetyNet also runs a Homeless Primary Care Team that brings healthcare into homeless services and outreach locations.

MQI provides health, addiction and harm-reduction supports at Merchant’s Quay. Current SafetyNet information lists GP clinics at MQI and outreach clinics at locations including the Lighthouse Café and Mendicity.

Using this app safely

No registration is required. Location is used only in this browser session to calculate distances and sort the list. The service directory is embedded in the file so it remains usable when mobile data is poor; the live map tiles and Google Maps navigation need a network connection.

If you are in immediate danger or have a serious medical emergency, call 112 or 999.
